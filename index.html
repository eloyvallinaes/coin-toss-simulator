How to read these results

Large effects show up quickly. If the two coins have very different true heads probabilities, even a small number of tosses can often detect a difference. With small samples, our estimate of the size of that gap will change a lot from one experiment to another. This is the same uncertainty that confidence intervals are designed to summarize.

Bigger samples reveal smaller effects. Increasing the number of tosses shrinks random noise. Tests with bigger samples are more likely to detect subtle differences between the two coins.

Small experiments can be misleading. Even when both coins are truly fair, short runs can make one coin look better just by chance. Equally fair coins can still produce nonzero observed gaps in individual experiments.
